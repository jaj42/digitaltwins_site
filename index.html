Digital twins for perioperative medicine

Part of

Every anaesthetic already produces a dense stream of signals — the heart, the vessels, the brain, the drugs going in. We build models personalised to the patient producing them, so that the monitor in the operating room stops reporting the present and starts anticipating what comes next.

The challenge

Surgery is safe enough that we stopped counting what it costs.

Anaesthesia is among the most common medical acts in the world, and one of the least examined. Between 15% and 25% of operations are followed by a complication — cognitive dysfunction, acute kidney injury, stroke, myocardial infarction — and the damage is often done during the procedure itself.

An anaesthetic that runs too deep bursts cerebral electrical activity and triples the incidence of delirium. Intraoperative hypotension impairs cerebral blood flow and injures myocardium. Both are visible on the monitors in the room, in signals nobody is modelling.

How do we improve monitoring in the operating room to mitigate postoperative complications?

Our approach

An unused nexus of data.

The operating room already records everything we need. It records it to a screen that forgets it a second later. We take that stream, keep it, and use it to fit a model of the patient that runs alongside them — a twin that can be asked what happens next, and what to do about it.

  1. Intraoperative data

    Vitals, EEG and drug administration, streamed out of the operating room and kept.

  2. Models

    Data management, biomarker design, organ-crosstalk modelling, and the digital twin itself.

  3. Augmented monitoring

    The model returns to the anaesthetist during the procedure, as feedback they can act on.

  4. Public health tool

    What the room learns about one patient becomes risk stratification for everyone.

Team

Every project has a doctor and a mathematician on it.

The team is built in duos — a medical researcher who knows what the operating room actually does, paired with a technology researcher who knows what the signal will support. Neither half writes a paper the other could not defend.
