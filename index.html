Digital twins for perioperative medicine

Part of

Every anaesthetic already produces a dense stream of signals — the heart, the vessels, the brain, the drugs going in. We build models personalised to the patient producing them, so that the monitor in the operating room stops reporting the present and starts anticipating what comes next.

Affiliations

The challenge

Surgery is safe enough that we stopped counting what it costs.

Anaesthesia is among the most common medical acts in the world, and one of the least examined. Between 15% and 25% of operations are followed by a complication — cognitive dysfunction, acute kidney injury, stroke, myocardial infarction — and the damage is often done during the procedure itself.

An anaesthetic that runs too deep bursts cerebral electrical activity and triples the incidence of delirium. Intraoperative hypotension impairs cerebral blood flow and injures myocardium. Both are visible on the monitors in the room, in signals nobody is modelling.

  • 300M

    anaesthetics given every year worldwide, 12 million of them in France.

  • 15–25%

    of operations are followed by a postoperative complication.

  • 7.7%

    of all deaths worldwide are postoperative — the third leading cause, after ischaemic heart disease and stroke.

Nepogodiev et al., The Lancet (2019); Pearse et al., The Lancet (2012).

How do we improve monitoring in the operating room to mitigate postoperative complications?

Our approach

An unused nexus of data.

The operating room already records everything we need. It records it to a screen that forgets it a second later. We take that stream, keep it, and use it to fit a model of the patient that runs alongside them — a twin that can be asked what happens next, and what to do about it.

  1. Intraoperative data

    Vitals, EEG and drug administration, streamed out of the operating room and kept.

  2. Models

    Data management, biomarker design, organ-crosstalk modelling, and the digital twin itself.

  3. Augmented monitoring

    The model returns to the anaesthetist during the procedure, as feedback they can act on.

  4. Public health tool

    What the room learns about one patient becomes risk stratification for everyone.

Research

From compartmental research to translational, multiscale research.

Current strategy

2023 – 2028

B1 — Infrastructure

Real-time compatible numerical infrastructure

Collect, store, analyse and return processed feedback while the operation is still happening. Physiological signals and drug data, harmonised on ingest and streamed rather than filed.

  • Data Warehouse Connect
  • Mirth
  • Kafka
  • TimescaleDB
  • Grafana
  • FastAPI

Target: physiological data from 50 operating rooms by 2025.

B2 — Monitoring

Advanced heart, brain and vessel monitoring

Model the crosstalk between organs under anaesthesia, tune the parameters against the database built over the past five years, and put the result behind an interface the anaesthetist can use: set a target effect, get a drug administration strategy for noradrenaline and propofol.

Outcome: an actionable model-based ecosystem for the effects of hypnotic and vasoactive agents.

B3 — Consultation

Medical consultation under general anaesthesia

General anaesthesia is a cardiovascular and cerebral stress test that millions of people already take. Read the organ response against the medical history, phenotype it, and stratify cardiovascular and cerebral risk from data that was collected anyway.

Outcome: making anaesthesia an instrument of public health.

Foundations

2017 – 2022

Four axes, built in order: the data first, then one organ system at a time.

Building a data collection platform

A2 — Heart

Intraoperative monitoring of cardiac function

The global afterload angle (GALA), a biomarker of global cardiac afterload read from aortic pressure against flow velocity. A non-invasive, continuous measure of pulse wave velocity from ECG and digital plethysmography. And a personalised cardiac digital twin that reproduces both a patient's baseline pressure–volume loops and the changes a drug induces.

  • EP3142549A1
  • EP22305129.3

Achieved: a cardiac model that reproduces the baseline and the response to pharmacological intervention.

A3 — Vessel

Augmented blood pressure monitoring

PlethoMAP reconstructs mean arterial pressure at high fidelity from non-invasive oxygen saturation alone, using a multi-threaded network over the photoplethysmography waveform — tracking the invasive gold standard between cuff inflations, where the standard non-invasive measure goes flat. Alongside it, a pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic model of norepinephrine's effect on blood pressure, validated on 29 prospectively recruited patients.

  • WO2019122406A1
  • 2021 Innovation Award

Achieved: high-fidelity arterial pressure, non-invasively.

A4 — Brain

Brain function under general anaesthesia

Transient alpha decrease (TAD), a modulation of the 7–14 Hz band, marks brain fragility under propofol and separates the patients who will develop burst suppression from those who will not. Separately, the alpha peak frequency shifts when cerebral perfusion is impaired — which today only a transcranial Doppler can establish, and no one runs one routinely.

  • Patent filed, under revision

Achieved: an EEG biomarker of brain fragility, and an EEG response to impaired cerebral blood flow.
